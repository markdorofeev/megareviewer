# The verdict and publishing

- **The reviewer picks the verdict when publishing:** comment (the default), approve or request changes. It no longer follows from counts of clicks, so Publish is offered on any complete analysis: an approval with nothing to say is a review in itself. Choosing was removed from the dialog once, when there was nothing to base it on without decisions; a raised finding is that base now. With silence meaning fine, the published review is the whole record of what the reviewer thought (see the fourth goal in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md)). Publishing again is another review, as on the host: a reviewer who commented can come back and approve once the author has answered. One review per analysis was the first version; after it the bar said only Published, and the way to approve was the host's own page, or a new push and a new analysis. On their own PR the verdict stays in the dialog, approve and request changes disabled, with a line saying the author can't use them; it was hidden there first, and a reviewer looking for Approve found nothing that said why. A comment with nothing in it isn't a review, so Publish waits for a summary, a line comment or a verdict, and says so. Approve is also a button of its own beside Publish, which approves at once with nothing else: an approval is the review a reviewer with nothing to say sends most, and the dialog made it three clicks. It's filled once Claude's run is done and nothing is pending, and an open finding doesn't block it, since saying nothing about a point means it's fine. Nor do new commits since the analysis: whether they need another look is the reviewer's call, so Approve only stays in outline while Re-analyze stands beside it. It was disabled until a new analysis before, which held up an approval over a push the reviewer didn't need megareviewer for. Beside the buttons the bar says the reviewer's own verdict on the host, Approved, Changes requested or Commented, however it was given, and nothing before there's one; "Published", which it said before, only said a review was sent, and an approval from the host's own page went unseen. Once the reviewer has approved, the bar is "Approved" and Revoke approval alone, until a comment waits and Publish comes back beside it: an approval is the whole review until there's more to say. Revoke takes it back at once, so an approval that a later push or a second look no longer earns isn't left for the host's page to undo. It's in outline, never filled, and works on an analysis the PR has moved past and on a merged or closed PR, since taking an approval back needs no fresh look at the code. After changes requested, Approve stays in outline: overturning the reviewer's own block shouldn't be the loudest thing on the row.
