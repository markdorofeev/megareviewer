# Points on the code and the landing

In the map, each point is on the part its lines are in, or else on the first part of the hunks it rests on, and its kind says where on it. A finding is a card under the last line it rests on, as a thread is, with a bug in that line's number cell: its title, one muted line of its severity, in its color, and its type, its text, the mismatch it shows and its suggested change as a diff, then Dismiss, Edit and Add to review. Six things before the title were the version before: a Finding label, type, severity and Draft chips, the lines' reference and "Only you see this". The title is what the reviewer reads; Draft and "Only you see this" said one thing twice, which Add to review says a third time; and the reference repeated the mark on the number above the card, so it's left to a card that isn't under its lines. Added to the review, the draft takes its text and suggestion but not its type or severity, which were Claude's, and the card folds to a line above the draft that holds it, and comes back if that draft is discarded. Once the comment is on the host, posted with a review or by Comment now, the card folds the same way above the thread, “Commented, below”: a raised point with no draft on its last line and a thread there that the reviewer started (`commentedPoints`), or anyone's when the host doesn't say who the reviewer is. The full card came back once the draft was gone before, and said the thread under it a second time. Taking the point off the page was the alternative, and left the pager and the counts with a finding fewer than the run found. A mismatch no finding names is the same card without either. The lines a point rests on are marked on their number beside the code: a tint, the number in its color and medium weight, and a 3 px rail on the code's side, the first point's where they overlap. The color is the finding's severity's, blocking red, major orange, minor amber, and grey for a mismatch; the card's bar, its severity's word, the bug and the landing's wash take it too, and a tour step's chips. The contrast check has each on every line tint. Every finding red was the version before, beside an amber severity chip that said it mattered little. The list the position opens is in the pager's order, each finding with its type and severity. A bar at the row's edge was the version before: about 115 px left of the code, it merged with the card's border and the focus bar, and on a range of one to three lines read as a tick. The edge bar is left to the focus and the open comment box. Two other forms were drawn and rejected: a caption line with a rail and no card, a fourth shape and quieter than something the reviewer is asked to check, and the lines themselves tinted with the note as their header, which replaces the diff's green or red on them. A point whose lines its part doesn't show sits under the part's header, and the other hunks a point rests on link to it, but not the other parts of the hunk its lines are in, which are around it. Dismiss folds a point to one line, with Undo. While risks and decisions were points, a risk was a lighter card of a finding's shell under its lines, with the test that checked it, and a decision a chip at the end of its line that opened a footnote (see [docs/design/findings.md](../design/findings.md)). Every point a card of one shape, with Raise and Dismiss, was the version before, first over its part and then under the last of its lines: on the fixture PR five cards sat on the receipt route, and the two findings read no louder than the three that could be left alone. A quiet list for risks and decisions under the findings' cards was tried and dropped: it kept all three kinds in one box. The designer's version had a teal band for logic code, a margin rail for decisions, Mark reviewed on risks and keys of their own; they were left out, for the reasons the page's elements record above. A tint under the code was the alternative to the mark: it would need a contrast pair for every syntax color on three more surfaces, for what a mark on the number beside a card that names its severity already says. Mechanical hunks are collapsed with their proof ("moved, verified identical"), and the user can expand them.

A point guidance shaped says so on its card, in a line of its own: “From your preference:” and the rule, or “From REVIEW.md”, a link to the preferences page and not to the preference, which may have been edited or deleted since the run kept its words. A point a Don't flag preference left out is folded to one line where its card would be, “Left out by your preference: Naming”, with Show. Folded, it isn't a step, isn't in the chips' or the tree's counts and marks no lines; shown, it's the point's card and behaves as any point. Shown is the page's own state: a reload folds it again unless it was raised or dismissed, because a new run reuses `f1` and a stored “shown” would open a point the reviewer never saw. Going to a left-out point from a link shows it first. When every point was left out the bar says so, “Claude found nothing to raise, and left out 2 by your preferences”, rather than that nothing was found while folded lines sit in the code. Dismiss has a menu beside it with “Don't flag things like this…”, which opens a form on the card in place of its actions: the kind fixed, the rule prefilled with the point's title for the reviewer to word, the scope the PR's repository first. Saving adds the preference and dismisses the point. The usage line names what the run read, “Sonnet 5.5 through Claude Code with 2 preferences and REVIEW.md”, and Run details lists each preference as the run had it, each guideline file with its size, and how many points were left out.

**Claude's run, the wait and the landing.** While Claude reads, a segment a third of the bar's width travels along a faint track on its bottom edge, and the code's brief stays under the title. When the intent pass finishes, the code's brief fades out and Claude's fades in where it was, the code's room kept until the run ends. Nothing else changes until the run has ended: the page shows the analysis as it was before the run, so findings that arrive first don't land alone and move the code under the reviewer twice. Then, about a second in all, once: the hairline runs out to the full width and fades; the pager and then the threads' chip come in one by one, 120 ms apart, each rising as its count rolls up from zero, then the split and what's missing; from 300 ms the tree's marks drop in row by row, the step shortened so 40 rows still start within 250 ms; from 600 ms each point whose lines are in the window, top to bottom, draws its mark down its lines' numbers, a wash of its color passes over them and fades away completely, and its card unfolds in height from under them. Cards out of the window are in place at once, in the same layout step that puts the line at the top of the window back where it was, so the code being read doesn't move and only cards in view unfold. The order is the reviewer's: what Claude found, where it is, then the code. It plays only when the page saw the run go from running to ended; opening an analyzed PR again shows everything at rest, and a card that's dismissed and undone, or scrolled to later, doesn't animate.

`useLanding` is the timeline's one owner. It sets `data-landing` on the page, `measure`, `landing`, then `done`, and gives each chip, tree row and card in view its place as `--landing-i`; CSS keyframes in `globals.css` play the rest, timed by motion tokens (`--landing-*`, `--hairline-*`) that are 0 ms under `prefers-reduced-motion`, which makes every step an instant change and sends the phase straight to `done`. `measure` is one render that's never painted: the page as it was, the new cards taking no room, to find which cards are in the window and note the top line (`holdTopLine`) before anything opens. The top line is a line of code found under the window's edge, below the sticky file header; holding the first file card in view was the version before, and missed when the reviewer was in the middle of a long file. When the run ends while the tab is hidden, its title says so until the reviewer comes back.

The wash passes and is gone, so the reason for a mark on the number rather than a tint under the code stands: nothing rests on a contrast pair the check doesn't cover. Cards growing in with a 4 px slide on every mount, and the tree's marks and chips appearing at once, was the first version: a card replayed its entrance when it was undone or reopened, and nothing said where to look first. A landing library was the alternative to CSS; the timeline is a few keyframes and custom properties, and a dependency for it would be the only one in the page that animates. A card above its lines was the version before; it pushed the lines it rests on down as it opened, so the reader watched lines light up and then move.
